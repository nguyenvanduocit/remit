# The "debt" family in AI-assisted software work: cognitive, comprehension, intent debt and their roots

Scope: definitions, origins, relations, remedies, theoretical roots (Naur, Clark), and whether "rebuild understanding on demand" holds up. Research date 2026-11-04. Tool budget: about 30 search/fetch calls. Read in full: Storey's arXiv paper (PDF v4, 8 pages), Gorman's 2025 post, the Spec Kit README, the Kiro specs docs, and Osmani's decision-log post. Also read in full: Clark's paper (Europe PMC full text). Not read in full: Naur's paper (read through a text transcription; the original scan has no text layer).

Confidence labels: HIGH = read in the primary source this session; MEDIUM = secondary source or partial primary; LOW = inference or unverified.

## 1. Definitions, origins and differences: cognitive vs comprehension vs intent debt; measurement proposals

### Takeaway
Storey's Triple Debt Model (arXiv 2603.22106, March 2026) gives the cleanest split. Technical debt lives in code, cognitive debt lives in people (shared understanding erodes), intent debt lives in missing artifacts (rationale, goals and constraints were never written down). "Comprehension debt" was coined by Jason Gorman (Codemanship, 2025-09-30) as the extra time needed to understand generated code before changing it. Osmani uses it interchangeably with cognitive debt, while Storey separates comprehension debt (individual) from cognitive debt (team-level, longitudinal). The brief credits "comprehension debt" to Jeremy Twei, but no source supports that. None of the sources proposes a validated metric. Storey names candidate measures (onboarding time, knowledge concentration, requirements coverage, intent-vs-behaviour audits) and Ahmad names others (explanation latency, modification difficulty).

### Cited Findings
- **Cognitive debt (Storey, blog, 2026-02-09):** understanding is lost when development outpaces the team's grasp of its system. "Technical debt lives in the code; cognitive debt lives in developers' minds." Storey does not claim to have coined the term. She says it is "gaining traction recently" and links an MIT Media Lab publication. HIGH. [Storey blog](https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/09/cognitive-debt/)
- Storey's example: a student team in an entrepreneurship course stalled in weeks 7–8. It first blamed technical debt, but "the theory of the system, their shared understanding, had fragmented or disappeared entirely". Storey explicitly invokes Naur's "a program is a theory", and also Brooks and Kent Beck. HIGH. [Storey blog](https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/09/cognitive-debt/)
- Storey's warning signs, all qualitative: team members are afraid to change code, knowledge sits with one or two people, and the system feels like a black box. She lists "how to measure it" as an open research question. HIGH. [Storey blog](https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/09/cognitive-debt/)
- **Triple Debt Model (Storey, arXiv 2603.22106, v1 2026-03-23, v4 2026-04-06; full PDF read):** three layers. "Technical debt lives in code… It limits how systems can change." "Cognitive debt lives in people… It limits how teams can reason about change." "Intent debt lives in artifacts… It limits whether the system continues to reflect what we meant to build." HIGH. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)
- Storey defines cognitive debt as "a team-level, project-level property". She separates her usage from (a) Kosmyna et al.'s "measurable reductions in individual neural engagement" (the MIT Media Lab EEG work) and (b) comprehension debt as "the growing gap between code developers can produce with AI and what they genuinely understand" [Alakmeh et al. 2026]. Hers is the team-level, longitudinal kind. So in Storey's own framing, comprehension debt is individual-level and cognitive debt is team-level. HIGH. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)
- Storey: "Software development has never required any one person to understand an entire system"; what matters is "sufficient, shared understanding across the team" (citing Curtis et al. 1988). What is new is "the rate at which this gap can accumulate". HIGH. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)
- **How the debts interact (Storey):** intent debt causes cognitive debt (newcomers cannot form accurate models without documented purpose); cognitive debt blocks paying down intent debt (people who lack understanding cannot write specs or decisions); cognitive debt causes technical debt; technical debt amplifies cognitive debt. Grounded in Hutchins' distributed cognition. HIGH. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)
- **Storey's diagnostics.** Cognitive debt: resistance to change; "unexpected results" (a change produces outcomes its author did not expect, which she calls "a reliable signal"); slow onboarding because docs say what, not why; loss of transactive memory (who knows what); low bus factor. Intent debt: behaviour drift found in testing or incidents; AI agents need extensive clarification, produce "technically correct but miss the point" solutions, or burn more tokens and time; loss of articulated non-functional constraints. HIGH. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)
- **Storey's measurement proposals (tentative):** "onboarding time tracking, knowledge concentration metrics, requirements coverage analysis, and regular audits of the gap between documented intent and actual behavior". These are named as directions, not validated metrics. HIGH. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)
- **Comprehension debt (Osmani):** "the growing gap between how much code exists in your system and how much of it any human being genuinely understands." It "breeds false confidence": the code looks clean and the tests pass. The post uses "comprehension debt" and "cognitive debt" interchangeably and does not attribute the term to Jeremy Twei or anyone else. HIGH. [Osmani, comprehension debt](https://addyosmani.com/blog/comprehension-debt/)
- Key Osmani line: "Making code cheap to generate doesn't make understanding cheap to skip. The comprehension work is the job." HIGH. [Osmani, comprehension debt](https://addyosmani.com/blog/comprehension-debt/)
- **Origin of "comprehension debt": Jason Gorman, Codemanship, 2025-09-30**, "Comprehension Debt: The Ticking Time Bomb of LLM-Generated Code". He writes: "When teams produce code faster than they can understand it, it creates what I've been calling 'comprehension debt'", and "The 'comprehension debt' is the extra time it's going to take us to understand it first." He also guesses the tool can fix its own code "maybe 70% of the time". That is his estimate, not data. HIGH (post read). [Gorman, Codemanship](https://codemanship.wordpress.com/2025/09/30/comprehension-debt-the-ticking-time-bomb-of-llm-generated-code/). Ahmad (EASE 2026) also credits Gorman. Secondary sources that credit Osmani or Arvid Kahl post-date Gorman. No result links Jeremy Twei to the term, so that attribution is unsupported. [Ahmad, arXiv 2604.13277](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13277); [The Note](https://thenote.app/post/en/comprehension-debt-what-ai-written-code-actually-costs-mjpyaqbowl)
- **Academic definition (Ahmad, Karlstad, EASE 2026):** comprehension debt is the gap between what a team knows about its codebase and what it needs to know to maintain and modify it. It lives in team cognition, stays hidden until cognitive demand exceeds understanding, and is grounded in Sweller's cognitive load theory, specifically the loss of germane load. HIGH. [arXiv 2604.13277](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13277)
- Ahmad's data: 621 reflective diaries from 207 students. He finds four patterns that accumulate debt (black-box acceptance, context-mismatch debt, dependency-induced comprehension atrophy, verification bypass) and one mitigating pattern (GenAI as a comprehension scaffold, for example rewriting generated code before committing). He proposes future indicators: **explanation latency** and **modification difficulty**, triangulated with repo activity, IDE logs and comprehension tests. These are proposals, not validated. HIGH. [arXiv 2604.13277](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13277)
- **Intent debt (Osmani, 2026-08-14, republished in O'Reilly Radar):** "the absence or erosion of the externalized rationale, goals, and constraints"; it "lives in artifacts". The three debts are independent: a codebase can have low technical debt and high intent debt, and one engineer can understand the code while the intent exists nowhere else. Osmani credits the three-part framework to Storey. HIGH. [Osmani, intent debt](https://addyosmani.com/blog/intent-debt/); [O'Reilly Radar](https://oreillyradar.substack.com/p/the-intent-debt)
- Osmani's asymmetry claim: agents can pay down technical debt (refactor) and cognitive debt (explain code), but "an agent can't generate intent". A model can only guess a plausible rationale, which he calls worse than admitting ignorance. Agents also raise the cost of unrecorded intent, because every new session starts without it. HIGH (as his claim). [O'Reilly Radar](https://oreillyradar.substack.com/p/the-intent-debt)
- Osmani's intent-debt symptoms: nobody knows whether a removed guard clause was load-bearing; behaviour users depend on changes while tests pass because they encode the old behaviour; a design's rationale is "An agent suggested it and it seemed fine." No metric is proposed. HIGH. [O'Reilly Radar](https://oreillyradar.substack.com/p/the-intent-debt)
- **Measured comprehension loss (Anthropic RCT, 2026-01-29, arXiv 2601.20245):** 52 mostly junior engineers learned the Trio library. The AI group scored about 50% on the comprehension quiz versus about 67% for controls (4.15 points on a 27-point scale, d=0.738, p=0.010). The speed gain was not significant. Low scorers (under 40%) relied heavily on AI for generation or debugging; people who asked conceptual follow-up questions scored higher. Note: the gap is 17 percentage points, about 25% relative, and some secondary sources misreport it as "17% lower". MEDIUM (secondary summaries agree; paper not read). [Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/02/ai-coding-skill-formation)

### Inferences
- The clean distinction is location. Technical debt sits in code, cognitive or comprehension debt sits in heads, intent debt sits in missing documents. "Cognitive" and "comprehension" debt are close to synonyms in practice. Storey frames hers as team-level shared understanding; Osmani and Gorman frame theirs as a code-volume versus understanding gap. MEDIUM.
- Intent debt is the only debt that cannot be repaid after the fact by reading the code. That is why it is the debt that matters most when agents write most of the code. MEDIUM (follows from Osmani's argument and the rationale study in §3).
- The nearest thing to a measure is the Anthropic quiz design (a comprehension test after a task) plus Ahmad's proposed explanation latency and modification difficulty. A practical team proxy could be: "can someone other than the author explain why X, without asking an agent?" LOW (my synthesis).

### Gaps
- No source supports the Jeremy Twei attribution. The report should not repeat it.
- The MIT Media Lab work is cited by Storey as Kosmyna et al.; the paper itself was not fetched.
- Alakmeh et al. 2026 (cited by Storey for comprehension debt) was not fetched.

## 2. Naur's "Programming as Theory Building" (1985) as the root theory

### Takeaway
Naur predicted cognitive and intent debt 40 years early. For him the program's essential part is a theory held by people, one that documentation cannot fully carry. When the team that holds it dissolves, the program "dies", and changes made without the theory make it decay. In AI terms, a codebase written by agents whose sessions end has a theory that lives nowhere: it is born dead in Naur's sense unless a human builds it or intent is externalized.

### Cited Findings
- The theory holder can (1) "explain how the solution relates to the affairs of the world", (2) "explain why each part of the program is what it is", and (3) "respond constructively to any demand for a modification of the program". HIGH (text transcription). [Naur transcription, gist](https://gist.github.com/onlurking/fc5c81d18cfce9ff81bc968a7f342fb1)
- "the program text and its documentation has proved insufficient as a carrier of some of the most important design ideas." HIGH. [gist](https://gist.github.com/onlurking/fc5c81d18cfce9ff81bc968a7f342fb1)
- "The death of a program happens when the programmer team possessing its theory is dissolved." HIGH. [gist](https://gist.github.com/onlurking/fc5c81d18cfce9ff81bc968a7f342fb1)
- Decay of program text follows from "modifications made by programmers without a proper grasp of the underlying theory." HIGH. [gist](https://gist.github.com/onlurking/fc5c81d18cfce9ff81bc968a7f342fb1)
- The theory "could not conceivably be expressed, but is inextricably bound to human beings." HIGH. [gist](https://gist.github.com/onlurking/fc5c81d18cfce9ff81bc968a7f342fb1)
- Caveat: the gist's last section ("Applying 'Theory Building'") is Cockburn's, not Naur's. HIGH. [gist](https://gist.github.com/onlurking/fc5c81d18cfce9ff81bc968a7f342fb1)
- Storey's paper calls the shared-understanding layer "what Naur called the 'theory of the system'". She proposes system walkthroughs "where developers explain code they did not write for the purpose of knowledge sharing and theory building [Naur 1985]". HIGH. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)
- Storey explicitly roots cognitive debt in Naur. Ahmad and Osmani's comprehension-debt post do not cite him. HIGH. [Storey blog](https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/09/cognitive-debt/); [arXiv 2604.13277](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13277)

### Inferences
- Map onto the triple: Naur's ability (2), "why each part is what it is", is exactly what intent debt destroys. Ability (3), modifying constructively, is what cognitive debt destroys. Ability (1), relating the program to the world, is what Storey's and the AGENTS.md "who is the customer" framing tries to externalize. MEDIUM.
- Naur sets a ceiling on intent-debt remedies. If the theory "could not conceivably be expressed", then ADRs and specs carry only part of it: the decisions, not the tacit sense of fit. Storey's paper effectively bets against strong Naur, holding that externalized rationale helps both humans and agents. That is a real theoretical tension the report should name. MEDIUM.
- "Theory lives nowhere": an agent session that wrote the code held a transient working theory in its context. When the session ends, the team holding the theory has "dissolved" in under an hour. Without a decision log, every later agent is a Naur-style reviver working from text alone. MEDIUM/LOW (analogy, not tested).

### Gaps
- Naur's text on program "revival" (that a new team cannot really revive a program from code and docs alone) was not quoted verbatim this session; the quote above covers only death. The original journal scan had no text layer.
- No empirical study was found that tests Naur's thesis directly in agent-written codebases.

## 3. Is "rebuild understanding on demand" viable?

### Takeaway
The Claude Code team states openly that nobody holds a full mental model and that understanding is rebuilt per slice through Claude-written explainers. They pair this with heavy deterministic and agentic guardrails (verification, multi-agent review, risk-tiered human approval, flags, rollback). The evidence supports on-demand rebuilding of WHAT the code does, at a cost. It does not support recovering WHY when the why was never written: LLMs then fabricate a plausible rationale. So the practice is viable only to the extent that intent is externalized and the guardrails catch what understanding misses.

### Cited Findings
- **Claude Code team (Osmani, Lunt, Wang, 2026-10-15):** "Nobody on the team keeps a complete mental model of Claude Code in their head, and we don't think anyone needs to." When someone needs to understand a part, "they ask Claude to write an explainer for exactly that slice". There is no formal process. HIGH. [claude.dev blog](https://claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/)
- Context from the same post: Claude writes about 90% of Claude Code. Review uses CLAUDE.md and REVIEW.md (the latter written by code owners), AI review on every PR verified by voting agents, a risk classifier that requires 0, 1 or 2 human approvals (sensitive paths always need a human), feature flags, latest-before-stable channels, and past incidents turned into a daily reviewer eval set. Merged PRs more than doubled from June to September while the revert share fell by more than 40%; PRs with substantive review comments went from 16% to 54%. HIGH (company self-report, not independent). [claude.dev blog](https://claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/)
- **The baseline cost of comprehension:** developers spend about 58% of their time on program comprehension (78 professionals, 7 projects, 3,148 hours); juniors spend a larger share than seniors. MEDIUM (abstract-level). [Xia et al., TSE 2018](https://xin-xia.github.io/publication/TSE17.pdf)
- **Knowledge loss when authors leave:** in 36,464 GitHub projects, 89.65% had at least one truck-factor developer detachment, most projects rely on one core developer, and only 27.07% of affected projects attracted new core developers and survived. HIGH. [Nourry et al., arXiv 2412.00313](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00313v1). The earlier Avelino et al. study (1,932 projects) found most projects do not survive TF detachment without replacement. MEDIUM. [Avelino et al. 2019, arXiv 1906.08058](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.08058.pdf)
- **Where rationale lives (Sun et al., W&M, arXiv 2604.10345, April 2026):** in 63 commits from 5 Java projects, only 9.7% of relevant sentences were rationale. The split: PRs 36.4%, commits 31.6%, issues 23.7%, code comments and Javadoc about 5.9%, code reviews 2.4%. Code itself carries almost none. No single artifact holds all rationale components. HIGH. [arXiv 2604.10345](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10345v1)
- **Same study, LLM rationale extraction:** precision 51.4% and recall 93.2% overall. Goal reached 76.6% precision; Need (39.2%) and Alternatives (15.4%) were much worse. **In 9 of 33 Need commits and 4 of 14 Alternatives commits the model produced a rationale summary where the ground truth had none.** That is direct evidence of fabricated why. HIGH. [arXiv 2604.10345](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10345v1)
- **Intent-level weakness:** on a commit-message versus diff inconsistency benchmark, models detected surface inconsistencies (component, file path, operation) better than intent-level "purpose" inconsistencies, which also had higher token cost. MEDIUM (search snippet). [CodeFuse-CommitEval, arXiv 2511.19875](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19875v1)
- **Hallucinated explanations of WHAT:** hallucination in code summaries is defined as a summary that does not accurately capture the intent and implementation of the code. Hallu-Eval (FSE 2026) found that perturbing code raised Qwen2.5-Coder-7B's hallucination rate from 17% to 97%; mitigation cut DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B's rate only from 66% to 59%. The ETF detector reaches 73% F1. These are small or mid-size open models, not frontier ones. MEDIUM. [Hallu-Eval FSE 2026](https://conf.researchr.org/details/fse-2026/fse-2026-research-papers/66/Hallucinations-in-LLM-based-Code-Summarization-Unveiling-Detection-and-Mitigation); [ETF, arXiv 2410.14748](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14748v2)
- **Passive vs active use:** in the Anthropic RCT, people who used AI to ask conceptual questions or to verify kept comprehension; people who delegated generation lost it. On-demand explainers resemble the conceptual-inquiry mode. MEDIUM. [Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)

### Inferences
- On-demand rebuilding can work for WHAT and HOW, because the code is the ground truth and frontier models read it well. Hallucination rates on small models, and their sensitivity to perturbation, are a warning that explainers need spot-checks against the code or tests. MEDIUM.
- It cannot work for WHY when the why was never externalized. The rationale study shows models invent Need and Alternatives where none exist. Osmani's "an agent can't generate intent" is consistent with this evidence. MEDIUM/HIGH.
- **Open question: does Storey's paper argue against explainer-per-slice?** The paper pulls both ways. Against: "Resist the automation of understanding". She warns that using AI to generate documentation produces "explanations of what the system does without the team ever building genuine understanding", substituting "the appearance of understanding for the real thing", and asks teams to be "cautious about any practice that produces the artifacts of understanding without hard cognitive work". For: the same section asks teams to invest "in tools and software design principles that help rebuild understanding"; her cognitive-debt practices include "reimplementation to repair cognitive debt", where teams "instruct an agent to reimplement a feature… that may help rebuild understanding"; and she writes that "no single individual needs to understand all aspects". Her target is AI-generated documentation that replaces the work of understanding. An explainer that a developer reads and interrogates before changing a slice fits her "tools… that help rebuild understanding". The paper does not discuss the explainer practice itself, so whether it conflicts depends on how the explainer is used, which the Claude Code post does not measure. HIGH for the quotes; the question stays open. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106); [claude.dev blog](https://claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/)
- Storey also writes that "no single individual needs to understand all aspects", and that only sufficient shared understanding matters. That supports the Claude Code team's "nobody keeps a complete mental model" as long as the slices are collectively covered. HIGH (quote). [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)
- The Claude Code example does not prove the strong claim "nobody needs a mental model". The team has CLAUDE.md and REVIEW.md written by code owners (externalized intent), risk-tiered human approvals, and incident-derived evals. It is "no single human holds the whole theory, but the theory is distributed across artifacts, reviewers and owners per path". That is closer to Clark than to pure delegation. MEDIUM.
- The truck-factor data is the base rate for what happens when theory holders leave: most projects do not recover. Agent-written code sets the truck factor near 0 for any slice nobody reviewed deeply, unless intent is externalized. LOW/MEDIUM (extrapolation).
- On-demand rebuilding shifts cost, it does not remove it. Comprehension is already about 58% of developer time; per-slice explainers make it cheaper and just-in-time, but unbounded "black box" regions accumulate between rebuilds. LOW.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-Anthropic) evaluation of the "explainer per slice" practice in a large professional codebase was found. I searched "LLM-generated code explanations developer comprehension onboarding unfamiliar codebase user study", and the nearest evidence is small and short-task:
  - Nam et al. (ICSE 2024, 32 participants): an in-IDE LLM explainer aided understanding of unfamiliar code better than web search.
  - A Sabancı 2025 thesis: an LLM onboarding tool for notebooks improved understanding and confidence.
  - A FLAIRS 2026 study: LLM explanations of intro-level code were largely accurate and improved novice comprehension.
  - A Twente MA thesis (Reefman), as cited by another paper: LLM tools cannot provide the business logic needed to understand a codebase. This matches the WHAT-vs-WHY split.

  MEDIUM (search summaries, not full texts). [Nam et al., arXiv 2307.08177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.08177v3.pdf); [Sabancı thesis](https://research.sabanciuniv.edu/id/eprint/53623/); [FLAIRS 2026](https://journals.flvc.org/FLAIRS/article/view/141647); [Reefman thesis](https://essay.utwente.nl/fileshare/file/103120/Reefman_MA_EEMCS.pdf)
- No study was found that measures frontier-model accuracy at *explaining* the intent behind existing code against developer-verified intent (searched "benchmark LLM infer developer intent code rationale accuracy"). The nearest is the reverse direction. DevIntent (arXiv 2608.07614, pilot, 49 HumanEval+ problems) finds Claude Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-4.1 pass over 92% of stated tests yet violate unstated developer intent in 54.5% and 63.5% of problems. Models do not infer unwritten intent when generating code either. MEDIUM (search summary of an early preprint). [DevIntent, arXiv 2608.07614](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07614)
- **Documentation decay (found on search):** Tan, Wagner and Treude (EMSE 2023, over 3,000 GitHub projects) found 28.9% of the top-1000 projects had at least one outdated code-element reference in their docs at analysis time, and 82.3% had been outdated at least once in their history. Such references typically stayed outdated for years before being fixed. This measures references to code elements, not rationale. Tan et al. cite Aghajani et al. 2019 for "up-to-dateness problems" as 39% of documentation content issues (original not checked). MEDIUM. [Tan et al., arXiv 2212.01479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.01479); [EMSE](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10664-023-10397-6)
- Results on whether the truck factor differs in AI-heavy repos were not found.

## 4. Remedies: ADRs, spec-driven development, agent decision logs, "list decisions and assumptions", delegation vs collaboration

### Takeaway
The remedies split by debt. Cognitive debt is answered by human-in-the-loop practices (someone understands each change, reviews, retros, pairing, using AI as a scaffold rather than a generator). Intent debt is answered by externalizing rationale at decision time (specs, ADRs, AGENTS.md as an intent ledger, agent-written decision logs, learnings files). Spec-driven tools (GitHub Spec Kit, Kiro) formalize the intent-first path. No remedy has outcome evidence yet beyond self-reports and small studies.

### Cited Findings
- **Storey's remedies:** at least one human fully understands each AI-generated change before it ships; document why, not just what; checkpoints (reviews, retros, knowledge sharing); slow down with pairing, refactoring and TDD; agent summaries help but do not replace human understanding. HIGH. [Storey blog](https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/09/cognitive-debt/)
- **Osmani on comprehension debt:** state what a change should do before writing it; treat verification as a structural constraint; keep a system-level mental model; separate "tests passed" from "I understand what this does and why". HIGH. [Osmani, comprehension debt](https://addyosmani.com/blog/comprehension-debt/)
- **Osmani on intent debt:** specs with goals, constraints, non-negotiables and a definition of done; AGENTS.md as an "intent ledger" holding conventions plus their reasons; ADRs or decision logs recorded at decision time; a learning loop where agents write root causes and failed attempts to a learnings file. HIGH. [O'Reilly Radar](https://oreillyradar.substack.com/p/the-intent-debt)
- **Ahmad (education):** AI-literacy instruction, comprehension-focused retrospectives, assessments that grade the ability to explain code as well as delivered features, and attention to automation bias. HIGH. [arXiv 2604.13277](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13277)
- **Storey's mitigation practices (full paper).** Cognitive debt: human code review and pairing to spread understanding; system walkthroughs where developers explain code they did not write; retrospectives and post-mortems "to help collectively rebuild mental models"; deliberate communication at implementation, onboarding and offboarding; and "reimplementation to repair cognitive debt" (have an agent reimplement a feature with different tests or design to rebuild understanding). Intent debt: "executable intent" (BDD specs and tests that capture purpose); ADRs ("what was decided, why, and what was not done", Nygard 2011) and DDD ubiquitous language; context artifacts (skills, agent instructions, playbooks) and AI-assisted intent capture from meetings. Her caveat: these "cannot replace the hard human work of making decisions". Her practice priorities: "Treat understanding as a deliverable", "Intent-first workflows", "Resist the automation of understanding", "Monitor the three layers in tandem". HIGH. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)
- **Osmani, "Automated Decision Logs in AI-Assisted Coding" (2024-11-26):** keep a project-local `fyi.md` or `ai_decisions.log` and prompt the AI to "keep a log of what, why and how you did what you did". "Verification is key: Don't trust, verify… It's your responsibility to ensure it's accurate." He hedges the benefits himself: onboarding helps only if the AI gives "useful explanations, and not just verbose summaries", and the log saves reverse-engineering the AI's intent "assuming that intent was coherent in the first place". HIGH. [Osmani, decision logs](https://addyosmani.com/blog/automated-decision-logs/)
- **GitHub Spec Kit (official README, fetched 2026-11-04):** "Define what to build and why, then turn the specification into a plan, tasks, and working code." The minimal flow: `/speckit-constitution` once per project, then `/speckit-specify`, `/speckit-plan`, `/speckit-tasks`, `/speckit-implement`, `/speckit-converge`, with the instruction to "Repeat implement and converge until convergence reports `Converged`". Skills run "one at a time, reviewing each result before continuing". The current README also documents a bug-fix flow (`/speckit-bug-assess`, `-bug-fix`, `-bug-test`) and an idea-assessment flow (`/speckit-assess-intake` … `-assess-decide`, producing a `decision.md` handoff, where stopping with a documented reason is a valid outcome). HIGH. [Spec Kit README](https://github.com/github/spec-kit) (fetched raw via raw.githubusercontent.com)
- **Kiro specs (official docs, kiro.dev/docs/specs):** "Every spec generates three key files": `requirements.md` (or `bugfix.md`) for user stories, acceptance criteria or bug analysis "in structured notation"; `design.md` for architecture, sequence diagrams, error handling and testing strategy; `tasks.md` for discrete, trackable tasks with real-time status as they run in the IDE, CLI or web. It is a three-phase workflow (requirements or bug analysis → design → tasks); bugfix specs record "current/expected/unchanged behavior". HIGH. [Kiro specs docs](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/)
- **Kiro spec modes (official docs):** feature specs have two variants. Requirements-First ("Requirements → Design → Tasks", for product-driven work) and Design-First (start from architecture or pseudocode, then "derive feasible requirements"). Quick Spec "runs all three phases automatically without approval gates between them" for well-understood features. `requirements.md` uses EARS notation: "WHEN [condition/event] THE SYSTEM SHALL [expected behavior]". HIGH. [Kiro feature specs](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/feature-specs/)
- **Kiro steering files (official docs):** `product.md` ("purpose, target users, key features, and business objectives… helps Kiro understand the 'why' behind technical decisions"), `tech.md` (stack and technical constraints) and `structure.md` (file organization, naming, "architectural decisions"). They are "included in every interaction by default". Team steering files can be distributed into `~/.kiro/steering`. This is an intent-ledger mechanism in Osmani's sense. HIGH. [Kiro steering](https://kiro.dev/docs/steering/)
- Note for the report: Quick Spec drops the human approval gates, so it trades away the point where a human builds understanding of the spec (Storey's cognitive-debt concern), while still producing intent artifacts. MEDIUM (inference).
- **ADR outcome evidence:** an MSR study of GitHub (IEEE Access 2023) found adoption is low though growing, and about half of ADR-using repos hold only 1–5 records. Systematic users write ADRs as a team over a long period. An ICSA 2026 paper (Santana Junior et al.) found about 63% of roughly 5,800 ADRs were opened directly as "accepted", skipping the proposal stage. Action research studies (Chalmers 2023; ECSA 2024, microservices) found teams perceive ADRs as useful and cooperation improves; the open concerns are documenting too much or too little and keeping records current. None is specific to AI-written code. MEDIUM (abstracts and search summaries, not full texts). [MSR study, JKU](https://research.jku.at/en/publications/using-architecture-decision-records-in-open-source-projects-an-ms/); [ICSA 2026](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icsa-2026/icsa-2026-papers/34/Architecture-Decision-Records-Adoption-Impact-and-Developer-Engagement-in-Open-Sou); [Chalmers action research](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/542849)
- **Agent-assisted rationale recovery (Argus):** LLM extraction of rationale from PRs, issues and commits was rated useful by 12 developers. Precision is low for Need and Alternatives, and the tool fabricates when no rationale exists. This supports capturing rationale at write time over recovering it later. HIGH. [arXiv 2604.10345](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10345v1)
- **Delegation vs collaboration evidence:** the Anthropic RCT found that passive delegation hurt comprehension, while question-driven, verify-oriented use preserved it. Ahmad found the same split, an "acceleration" orientation (AI as solution provider) versus an "exploration" orientation (AI as scaffold). GenAI amplifies whichever orientation the user already has. MEDIUM/HIGH. [Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills); [arXiv 2604.13277](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13277)

### Inferences
- "Ask the agent to list the decisions and assumptions it made" is the cheapest intent-debt remedy. It turns the session's transient working theory into an artifact before the session dies (Naur's "team dissolved"). No study measured it; it is consistent with the rationale-study finding that the why must be captured at write time. LOW/MEDIUM.
- Spec-driven development addresses intent debt but can create a new failure mode: specs that drift from the code and become a second, stale source of truth (the documentation-decay problem moved upstream). No source measured this. LOW.
- Remedies map to Naur's abilities: specs and ADRs carry (1) and part of (2); the "one human understands each change" rule and per-path code owners preserve (3). MEDIUM.

### Gaps
- ADR studies exist (see above) but measure adoption and perceived usefulness, not defect or comprehension outcomes. No empirical study was found of agent-written decision logs (`fyi.md` style) or of spec-driven development outcomes in AI-heavy teams. ## 5. Andy Clark's extended mind as the counterweight: when is offloading cognition rather than debt?

### Takeaway
Clark (Nature Communications 2025, full text read) argues that humans are natural builders of hybrid thinking systems, so lost on-board skill is not automatically a loss. The real questions are practical: is the external store fragile, is what it returns false or misleading, would biological memory be more reliable here? He splits work three ways: what can be "devolved entirely", what stays in biological memory, and what "must emerge through… epistemically well-regulated interaction". He calls the needed metacognitive skill "extended cognitive hygiene". Together with his older trust-and-glue criteria (the resource is reliably available, trusted, easy to access), this gives a test: offloading is part of cognition when the external store is reliable, trusted and accessible when needed. It becomes debt when it fails those conditions, for example an explanation that hallucinates, or a rationale that was never stored.

### Cited Findings
- Paper: Andy Clark, "Extending Minds with Generative AI", *Nature Communications* 16, 4627 (published 2025-05-19), DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-59906-9, PubMed 40389440. HIGH (bibliographic). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40389440/)
- Full text read via Europe PMC (PMC12089268, CC BY-NC-ND). Abstract: "As human-AI collaborations become the norm, we should remind ourselves that it is our basic nature to build hybrid thinking systems – ones that fluidly incorporate non-biological resources." HIGH. [Europe PMC full text](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12089268); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40389440/)
- Clark rejects the frame of "simple 'offloadings'": tool use creates "delicately interwoven new wholes - brain, body, world tapestries". Lost on-board skill is "intrinsically bad… only if you start off by identifying your mind and self with… your bare biological brain"; otherwise it can be "the careful husbanding of our own on-board cognitive capital". HIGH. [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12089268)
- **Clark's test for when offloading becomes a problem:** "the real worries are more practical ones. Perhaps the online storage… is fragile or corrupt? Perhaps the information you retrieve is likely to be false or misleading? Perhaps bio-storage would actually… be cheaper, better, or more reliable". He calls these "exactly the right kinds of question to learn to ask". HIGH. [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12089268)
- **Clark's three-way split, the closest he comes to "delegation vs collaboration":** work will divide into "what can safely be devolved entirely to the new digital unconscious, what remains apt for storage in biological memory, and what must emerge through new kinds of epistemically well-regulated interaction." He does not use the words "delegation vs collaboration"; that framing is the brief's. HIGH (quote) / MEDIUM (mapping). [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12089268)
- **Metacognition as the core skill:** "skills of knowing what to rely upon and when"; "We now need to become experts at estimating the likely reliability of a response given both the subject matter and our level of skill at orchestrating a series of prompts"; we should "both trust and question" AI suggestions as we would a thought from our own unconscious. "Deep and abiding concerns for 'extended cognitive hygiene' will need to be instilled in us from quite an early age." HIGH. [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12089268)
- Clark's earlier trust-and-glue criteria are trustworthiness, reliability and accessibility (Clark 2008; 2010). A 2025 critic (Oliveira) proposes adding **maintainability** for human-AI coupling. MEDIUM. [SciELO, Trans/Form/Ação](https://www.scielo.br/j/trans/a/W4HKYsLfCF6PGjsDbcCDs3h/)
- Commentary on Clark: the smoother the coupling, the harder it is to monitor the external component's reliability. LOW (non-academic summary). [You On AI field guide](https://www.youonai.ai/fieldguide/txt/andyclark.html)
- A related framework attributes to Clark the idea of practices "to ensure that externally delegated cognition contributes to rather than degrades the internal system", and distinguishes assistive, substitutive and disruptive offloading. That taxonomy belongs to the framework's authors, not Clark. MEDIUM. [Doom Researching, arXiv 2607.02723](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02723)

- **Storey draws the same line from the debt side:** she contrasts "cognitive surrender", from Shaw and Nave 2026 ("adopting AI outputs with minimal scrutiny, bypassing both intuition and deliberate reasoning"), with "cognitive offloading, the strategic delegation of a discrete task to a tool (using a linter, a type checker, a spell-checker)… a rational productivity choice." She reports that Shaw and Nave find surrender "inflates confidence even when the AI is wrong". She also grounds the model in Hutchins' distributed cognition, which treats cognition as a property of "systems of people, artifacts, and their interactions", close to Clark's extended-mind view. HIGH. [arXiv 2603.22106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22106)

### Inferences
- Storey's offloading/surrender distinction and Clark's trust-and-glue criteria agree on where the line falls. Delegating a bounded task to a reliable tool whose output is checked is legitimate extended cognition. Accepting output unscrutinized, or relying on a store that does not exist (unrecorded intent), is debt. MEDIUM.
- Clark reconciles with the debt view through his own criteria. Offloading the WHAT to an on-demand explainer can pass trust and glue: the code is always there, and frontier explainers are fairly reliable, so this is legitimate extended cognition and not debt. Offloading the WHY to a model fails the reliability criterion, because the model fabricates rationale when none is stored (§3). So intent debt is exactly offloading onto a store that does not exist. MEDIUM.
- Clark's position strengthens the Claude Code team's stance ("nobody holds the whole model") only if the hybrid system includes reliable stores of intent (CLAUDE.md, REVIEW.md, owners per path). Without those, it is the "substitutive or disruptive" kind of offloading the Anthropic RCT measured as comprehension loss. MEDIUM.
- Oliveira's proposed maintainability criterion fits software well. A store of intent that is not kept true as the code changes (stale ADRs, drifting specs) fails it, and that failure is the documentation-decay problem. LOW/MEDIUM.
- The tension with Naur: Naur says the theory is "inextricably bound to human beings"; Clark says the boundary of the mind is not the skull. A defensible synthesis is that the theory can be distributed across people and artifacts, but some human or reliable store must hold each part, or that part is dead. LOW (philosophical synthesis).

### Gaps
- Clark's paper is a short perspective piece with no empirical data on software teams. Applying it to codebases is the report's inference.
- No empirical work was found that applies trust-and-glue criteria to developer use of coding agents.
