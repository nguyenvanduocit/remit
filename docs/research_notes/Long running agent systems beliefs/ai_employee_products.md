## 1. What is the human's role, how autonomous should agents be today, and what bet do they make on future models?

### Takeaway
Every coding-agent vendor in this set has moved the human from writing code to **defining the problem, setting review criteria and reviewing artifacts** ("manager of a fleet"). They differ on where accountability sits. Linear says it stays with a named human. GitHub keeps a human approval gate before CI or merge. Cognition, Cursor, Factory and Amp bet that models will soon need little supervision, and Factory and Amp use "dark factory" and "without supervision" language. The sales "AI employee" vendors (11x, Artisan, Lindy) launched on replacing human roles. 11x's record shows that framing set expectations the product did not meet, and Artisan has since restated its belief as "humans and AI, not humans or AI".

### Cited Findings
**Cursor (Michael Truell, "third era", 2026-02-26)**
- Three eras: tab autocomplete → synchronous agents → "agents that can tackle larger tasks independently, over longer timescales, with less human direction." — [Cursor blog](https://cursor.com/blog/third-era) (HIGH)
- "The human role shifts from guiding each line of code to defining the problem and setting review criteria." Developers "spend their time breaking down problems, reviewing artifacts, and giving feedback" and "spin up multiple agents simultaneously instead of handholding one to completion." — [Cursor blog](https://cursor.com/blog/third-era) (HIGH)
- Cursor's stated product goal is "helping developers build the factory that creates their software", made of "fleets of agents that they interact with as teammates." — [Cursor blog](https://cursor.com/blog/third-era) (HIGH)
- Model bet: "A year from now, we think the vast majority of development work will be done by these kinds of agents." Internal claim: 35% of PRs merged at Cursor are created by autonomous agents in cloud VMs. — [Cursor blog](https://cursor.com/blog/third-era) (HIGH for the claim being made; the number is self-reported)
- Cursor 3 (2026-04-02) replaced the editor-first UI with an agent-first workspace: all local and cloud agents sit in one sidebar and run in parallel across repos. — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/04/cursor-3-agent-first-interface/) (MEDIUM)

**OpenAI Codex**
- Humans steer "at milestones instead of micromanaging every line", which frees them for "design, architecture, product decisions". Goal: "make the agent feel less like a tool you babysit and more like a teammate you can trust on long-horizon work." — [OpenAI developers blog, "Run long horizon tasks with Codex"](https://developers.openai.com/blog/run-long-horizon-tasks-with-codex) (HIGH; the page carries no date or author and refers to GPT-5.2 (Dec 2025) and GPT-5.3-Codex, so LOW on the date, about early 2026)
- Demonstration: "Codex ran for about 25 hours uninterrupted, used about 13M tokens, and generated about 30k lines of code". The post frames this as an experiment and stress test, not a production rollout. — [same](https://developers.openai.com/blog/run-long-horizon-tasks-with-codex) (HIGH)

**Factory (Matan Grinberg, Sequoia Training Data, published 2026-11-11)**
- Mission: "our mission is to bring autonomy to software engineering." Vision: "a dark factory, where the lights are off and things are just happening". — [Sequoia podcast](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/factorys-matan-grinberg-the-coming-dark-factory-where-software-builds-itself) (HIGH)
- "fully autonomous agents require a complete change in behavior from the developer"; you want people "to own an outcome end to end". — [same](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/factorys-matan-grinberg-the-coming-dark-factory-where-software-builds-itself) (HIGH)
- Model bet: he expects usage to "asymptote towards the vast majority being open" models, because they are "faster and … cheaper". — [same](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/factorys-matan-grinberg-the-coming-dark-factory-where-software-builds-itself) (HIGH)
- "Factory 2.0: From coding agents to software factories" (Grinberg and Eno Reyes, 2026-06-15): "No organization starts with a fully autonomous software factory." Autonomy is "a maturation process that is gradual and specific to every organization's readiness and comfort level". Engineers "will be responsible for building the factories that build the software", which brings responsibility for governance, safety and business outcomes. "Improving the productivity of individual engineers is no longer enough." Missions are multi-agent execution "over hours or days". — [Factory](https://factory.com/news/software-factory) (HIGH)
- Factory publishes an Autonomy Maturity Model: five levels from manual execution to a self-sustaining factory, scored on eight technical pillars, with readiness exposed through the Droid CLI, dashboard and API. — [Factory maturity-model page](https://factory.com/maturity-model/factory-autonomy-maturity-model-whitepaper.pdf) (MEDIUM: the page confirms "The Five Maturity Levels" and "The Eight Pillars" exist but does not list them; the level and pillar details come from a search snippet). A search snippet also reported that 14% of Missions run longer than 24 hours and the longest ran 16 days; that page does not contain these numbers (LOW, unverified).
- A secondary summary has Grinberg predicting that most coding-agent tokens become asynchronous within 12–24 months and that pricing moves toward outcomes. — [BigGo summary](https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/5be84fe394283726) (MEDIUM/LOW, secondary)

**Amp (Sourcegraph spin-out; Quinn Slack, Thorsten Ball)**
- "By keeping these new models in an editor sidebar, we restrict them." Models "want to write code and run even when you're not sitting in front of your editor … It's time to see what they can do without supervision." — [Amp, "The Coding Agent Is Dead", 2026-02-19](https://ampcode.com/news/the-coding-agent-is-dead) (HIGH)

- Product as direction, not fixed form: "Today Amp ceases to be a coding agent. It's no longer a point on a map. It's an arrow." The CLI is "a ladder" and "might self-destruct too". — [same](https://ampcode.com/news/the-coding-agent-is-dead) (HIGH)

**Cognition (Devin)**

Cold calling stays human ("AI is not good at and probably shouldn't be"; legally AI cannot cold call), so Artisan built a dialer for reps. "Companies shouldn't use AI to get the same output from a fifth of the team. They should get five times the output from the team they already have." It claims customers' GTM teams grow, and its largest deployment runs Ava alongside more than 1,000 human reps. - Lindy launched (2023) on the claim that AI employees are much faster, much cheaper and available 24/7/365. ### Inferences
- MEDIUM: Coding vendors converge on "human = manager and reviewer of artifacts", but they split on **where accountability sits**. Linear and GitHub encode a human gate in data (assignee, approval button). Cursor, Factory and Amp describe direction but encode no named accountable person in what was read.
- MEDIUM: The future-model bet is uniform: each vendor expects scaffolding (sidebar, IDE, wrapper prompts) to fall away. Amp says so most bluntly ("bash is often enough"). Factory adds a contrarian bet on open models.
- MEDIUM: "AI employee" framing that replaces a whole role hurts when the product cannot absorb that role: 11x's churn was attributed to customers hoping it "could replace an entire outbound sales team", and Artisan retired its slogan and now splits work by task (volume outreach to AI, calls to humans). The coding vendors framing the agent as a "teammate" with a human owner avoid this.

## 2. How do they model an agent (tool, teammate, employee, swarm)? Single agent or multi-agent, and why?

### Takeaway
The clearest public intellectual arc is Cognition's: "Don't Build Multi-Agents" (June 2025) → "Devin can now Manage Devins" (March 2026) → "Multi-Agents: What's Actually Working" (April 2026). The 2026 synthesis, "writes stay single-threaded and the additional agents contribute intelligence rather than actions", is close to an industry consensus. Reviewers, managers that spawn isolated children, and read-only subagents work. Parallel writers and swarms do not.

### Cited Findings
- Cognition 2025 principles: (1) "Share context, and share full agent traces, not just individual messages"; (2) "Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results." The Flappy Bird example has two subagents producing a Mario-style background and a mismatched bird. Recommendation: a single-threaded linear agent, plus a compression model for long histories. The author hedged: "we expect things to change as the field advances". — [Walden Yan, Cognition, 2025-06-12](https://cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents) (HIGH)
- Cognition 2026-03-19: in one long session "context accumulates, focus degrades, and the quality of each subtask suffers". The coordinator "assigns each piece to a managed Devin, monitors progress, resolves any conflicts, and compiles the results". Each child runs in its own VM with its own shell and test runner, and can "verify its own changes before reporting back". — [Cognition, "Devin can now Manage Devins"](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-can-now-manage-devins) (HIGH)
- Cognition 2026-04-22: "writes stay single-threaded and the additional agents contribute intelligence rather than actions". Working patterns are generator–verifier (Devin Review, "an average of 2 bugs per PR"; "clean context leads to a notable improvement in capabilities"), manager ("map-reduce-and-manage"), and "smart friend" (a cross-frontier model consult, which "works today when both models are strong"). Not working: "Agents assume they share state with their children when they don't"; cross-agent messaging "doesn't happen by default, because models haven't been trained in environments where it needed to"; unstructured swarms are "mostly a distraction". "The open problems are all communication problems." Read-only subagents "mostly resemble tool calls rather than true multi-agent collaboration." — [Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working) (HIGH)
- Cognition 2026-06-29, "Devin Fusion": a main agent paired with a smaller "sidekick" model running in parallel, claimed up to 60% cheaper on their FrontierCode 1.1 Extended benchmark. — [Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-fusion) (MEDIUM; snippet, self-reported benchmark)
- Cursor: agents are "teammates" in "fleets", each on its own VM; the developer runs many in parallel. — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/third-era) (HIGH)
- Factory: "Droids" as named, specialised agents; "Missions" for multi-agent, multi-hour or multi-day work. — [Sequoia Training Data (earlier Factory episode)](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/training-data-factory) (MEDIUM)
- Linear: an agent is a first-class workspace member, but must "disclose that it's an agent", "inhabit the platform natively" (do the same actions a person could), give "instant feedback", and be "clear and transparent about its internal state". — [Linear AIG](https://linear.app/developers/aig) (HIGH)
- Amp: one product, threads that "work the same way everywhere" (phone, browser, CLI). Quinn Slack: "The #1 complaint I hear about other agents is that local vs. remote threads … are all different underlying impls." — [Quinn Slack on X](https://x.com/sqs/status/2071974918129176841) (MEDIUM). In 2025 Amp had no model selector and chose models itself. — [Shantanu Goel review, 2025-06](https://shantanugoel.com/2025/06/15/ampcode/), [hamel.dev notes](https://hamel.dev/notes/coding-agents/amp.html) (MEDIUM). The current manual says "Amp uses them all, for what each model is best at" and has a Model Selector, so the strict no-picker stance no longer holds. Stated philosophy: "If we don't use and love a feature, we kill it"; "Amp goes where the models take it"; no backward compatibility or legacy features. — [Amp manual](https://ampcode.com/manual) (HIGH). Amp's docs on models and subagents: the Oracle (a plugin, `@amp/oracle`) "reviews code and advises on hard decisions, and it never edits files". The write-capable Task subagent "has access to tools like file editing and terminal commands". Subagents are isolated: "they can't communicate with each other, you can't guide them directly", and they suit "multi-step tasks that can be broken into independent parts" while "keeping the main thread's context clean". The user picks the main model and effort in the Model Selector; the default "Staff Pick" is "the model the Amp team would choose for most work". "System Models" handle side jobs (image analysis, thread naming, compaction) so Amp can use "a frontier model where deep reasoning matters and a faster specialist where speed matters more". — [Amp docs, Models & Subagents](https://ampcode.com/docs/models-and-subagents) (HIGH). The read-only Oracle matches Cognition's "smart friend" pattern. — MEDIUM, inference
- OpenAI Codex: a single agent per thread, with parallel threads across projects in the app. — [OpenAI developers blog](https://developers.openai.com/blog/run-long-horizon-tasks-with-codex) (HIGH)
- Jules "Planning Critic" (2026-01-26): a second agent reviews plans that run without human sign-off, before any code is written. — [Jules changelog 2026-01-26](https://jules.google/docs/changelog/2026-01-26-1) (MEDIUM)
- Lindy Teammate is described as "multiplayer": one agent shared by a team in Slack. — [Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/lindy-teammate-flo-crivello-on-multiplayer-agents-memory-why-he-d-ban-the-chinese-models-he-uses/) (MEDIUM)

### Inferences
- HIGH (well supported across sources): the 2026 pattern is one writer per isolated workspace. Extra agents are verifiers (Devin Review, Jules Planning Critic), consultants (smart friend, Oracle) or coordinated children in isolated VMs. No vendor read here promotes shared-state swarms.
- MEDIUM: "Teammate" is the dominant noun among coding vendors (Cursor, OpenAI, Linear). "Employee" survives mainly in go-to-market products (Lindy, Artisan, 11x, Relevance).

### Gaps
- Factory's internal Missions architecture (how parallel tracks avoid conflicting writes) is not described in the Factory 2.0 post.
- No primary GitHub statement on single versus multi-agent was found.

## 3. How do they handle trust, review, permissions and stopping a long-running agent?

### Takeaway
Trust is built from three things: (a) an isolated sandbox per agent (VM or container), (b) verification artifacts the human can review fast (tests, logs, video, previews, an AI reviewer), and (c) a hard human gate at merge or CI (GitHub, Linear accountability). Stop or disengage is a stated principle only at Linear. Most vendors talk about review, not interruption.

### Cited Findings
- Linear AIG Principle 05: "An agent should respect requests to disengage": when asked to stop, it withdraws immediately and resumes only after explicit permission. Principle 04: show whether it is thinking, waiting, acting or finished, and let people inspect reasoning and actions. — [Linear AIG](https://linear.app/developers/aig) (HIGH)
- GitHub Copilot coding agent: tokens scoped "to only the code repository in which it operates"; internet "tightly limited to a trusted list of destinations that you can customize"; only comments from users with write permission are processed; no workflows run before human approval; the agent cannot approve or merge. — [GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/copilot/concepts/agents/coding-agent/about-coding-agent), [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/product-news/github-copilot-meet-the-new-coding-agent/) (MEDIUM, snippets)
- Cursor: agents return "something quickly reviewable: logs, video recordings, and live previews rather than diffs", so reviewers can judge output "without reconstructing each session from scratch". — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/third-era) (HIGH)
- OpenAI Codex: trust comes from a spec that freezes the target ("so the agent doesn't 'build something impressive but wrong'"), a plan with checkpoints, and "Run validation after each milestone (fix failures immediately)"; "Codex did not just write code and hope it worked." — [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/blog/run-long-horizon-tasks-with-codex) (HIGH)
- Cognition: AI-on-AI review (Devin Review iterates against Devin before a human looks), and child sessions are directly inspectable: "Each has its own session link, so you can inspect its work or message it directly." — [Cognition 2026-04-22](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working); [Cognition 2026-03-19](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-can-now-manage-devins) (HIGH)
- Factory: validation is test-driven: "Write me code, and here's how I know it was done well"; "you need to make sure your tests are very thorough". — [Sequoia 2026-11-11](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/factorys-matan-grinberg-the-coming-dark-factory-where-software-builds-itself) (HIGH)
- Jules: the official API docs say "By default, sessions created through the API will have their plans automatically approved", and "If you want to create a session that requires explicit plan approval, set the `requirePlanApproval` field to `true`." — [Jules API docs](https://developers.google.com/jules/api) (HIGH). Since 2026-01-26 a "Planning Critic" agent reviews every plan that does not require human intervention. — [Jules changelog](https://jules.google/docs/changelog/2026-01-26-1) (MEDIUM). This resolves the earlier conflict: the human approval gate is opt-in, and AI review is the default. — [rywalker.com review](https://rywalker.com/research/jules) agrees (MEDIUM)
- Relevance AI makes approval a per-edge setting: select the edge between an agent and a tool (or another agent) and choose "Approval required"; "The agent will now draft its intended action and wait for human approval before proceeding." A "Let Agent Decide" mode tells the agent to "Request approval when facing uncertainty or high-risk situations." The escalation path goes "to specific human roles or other agents". A pending task "will remain in a pending state until approved or rejected", with configurable timeout actions. — [Relevance AI docs, Approvals and escalations](https://relevanceai.com/docs/build/workforces/workforce-features/approvals-and-escalations) (HIGH)
- Stopping and caps differ by vendor:
  - GitHub Copilot cloud agent (Actions-based sessions) "has a maximum execution time of 59 minutes. This limit cannot be extended or bypassed. If a task exceeds the limit, the session stops." It can work on "one branch at a time and can open one pull request per task"; without write access the session is read-only. — [GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/agents/cloud-agent/about-cloud-agent) (HIGH)
  - Devin has a per-message ACU limit, separate from the overall session cap: "the limit at which devin would stop working after each user message". "Once Devin hits the session limit, he will go to sleep and must be woken up again to resume." It exists to stop loops that waste ACUs. — [Cognition support article](https://knowledge.cognition.ai/articles/1715208244-for-people-confused-about-session-credit-limits) (HIGH for the text; the page says only "Last updated 1 year ago")
  - Cursor: a user asked on 2026-04-21 for a "hard stop" at some runtime for automations, and no staff reply is shown; a second request (2026-06-05) says Automations "can run longer or cost more than expected when an agent gets stuck in a loop, waits indefinitely" and asks that a run "stop cleanly and report that it was terminated by the limit"; no staff reply is shown on either. — [Cursor forum, hard stop](https://forum.cursor.com/t/hard-stop-for-long-running-cloud-agents/158635), [Cursor forum, time or cost limit](https://forum.cursor.com/t/time-or-cost-limit-for-automations/162483) (HIGH that the requests exist; MEDIUM inference that no per-run cap had shipped by June 2026)
  - Codex: no documented wall-clock cap for cloud tasks was found. Usage is metered by a rolling 5-hour usage window shared by local and cloud tasks, and users report cloud tasks being terminated when that window runs out. — [OpenAI community forum](https://community.openai.com/t/codex-cloud-used-my-entire-5-hour-limit-in-two-c-tasks-bug-or-policy-change/1364874), [Codex pricing page](https://developers.openai.com/codex/pricing.md) (MEDIUM/LOW; snippets, user reports)
- Outside critique: practitioners name review capacity as the bottleneck of background agents. An HN commenter proposes capping how many AI PRs sit in review at once. Nader's "Cloud Agent Thesis" says that at scale without automated review "you're either rubber-stamping PRs or drowning in review debt". A 2026 Devin review says it is an async agent you assign tickets to, "not a humanoid replacement for your team". — [HN comments (afro88)](https://news.ycombinator.com/threads?id=afro88), [Nader, The Cloud Agent Thesis](https://nader.substack.com/p/the-cloud-agent-thesis), [DEV Community Devin review](https://dev.to/pickuma/devin-by-cognition-review-2026-is-the-autonomous-ai-engineer-worth-it-mbc) (MEDIUM/LOW; snippets only, not fetched)

### Inferences
- MEDIUM: The industry is moving from "human approves the plan" (Jules 2025, early Devin) to "AI reviewer plus human reviews artifacts at the end" (Cognition, Cursor). Plan approval is being automated: Jules auto-approves by default and puts an AI critic in place of the human.
- MEDIUM: Interruption is handled through limits, not principles. GitHub uses a hard wall-clock cap, Devin a per-message budget after which the agent sleeps until woken, and Cursor users were asking for a runtime stop in 2026. Only Linear states "stop when asked" as a principle.

### Gaps
- Codex cloud and Cursor cloud agents: no official doc on a per-run runtime or cost cap was found; the evidence is user requests and forum reports.
- Agent HQ governance details rest on a third-party page.

## 4. How do agents learn or keep memory across tasks?

### Takeaway
Two families. **Repo-resident memory** (AGENTS.md, spec, plan and status files committed to the codebase) is favoured by OpenAI, Factory and Amp. **Product-resident memory** (Devin Knowledge and Playbooks, Lindy's accumulated team context) lives in the vendor's store. Cognition adds learning from trajectories: a manager reads children's full traces to decompose better.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI: "The most important technique was durable project memory." The status file is a shared memory and audit log ("It's how I can step away for hours and still understand what happened"). "Plans markdown file is source of truth (milestone-by-milestone)". — [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/blog/run-long-horizon-tasks-with-codex) (HIGH)
- Factory: "so much tribal knowledge can be codified"; "the work that we've done stays in your codebase". — [Sequoia 2026-11-11](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/factorys-matan-grinberg-the-coming-dark-factory-where-software-builds-itself) (HIGH)
- Amp: the bottleneck is "How you organize your codebase for agents", which implies memory and leverage live in the repo. — [Amp](https://ampcode.com/news/the-coding-agent-is-dead) (HIGH quote; MEDIUM inference)
- Cognition: the manager can "read the full trajectories of its managed Devins to understand what worked, what didn't, and where they got stuck", which improves how it breaks down later tasks. — [Cognition 2026-03-19](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-can-now-manage-devins) (HIGH)
- Cognition 2025: proposes a dedicated compression model to condense long action and conversation history into "key details, events, and decisions". — [Cognition 2025-06-12](https://cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents) (HIGH)
- Devin Knowledge: "a collection of tips, advice, and instructions that Devin can reference in all sessions". Each item has a trigger description; "Devin retrieves Knowledge when relevant, not all at once or all at the beginning." "Devin will automatically suggest Knowledge to remember based on your feedback in chat." Scope is per organization or enterprise, and items can be pinned to one repo, all repos or none. The docs now mark Knowledge as deprecated, migrating to Skills, which are recommended for new instructions. Playbooks are reusable task prompts. — [Devin docs, Knowledge](https://docs.devin.ai/product-guides/knowledge) (HIGH; the Playbooks page itself was not opened)
- Factory 2.0: every stage of the lifecycle is instrumented so agent sessions, code reviews and resolved incidents feed back into the loop, and "The router itself learns to optimizes resources" (sic). — [Factory, 2026-06-15](https://factory.com/news/software-factory) (HIGH)
- Lindy Teammate "accumulates your entire team's context"; Crivello discusses memory in multiplayer agents. — [Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/lindy-teammate-flo-crivello-on-multiplayer-agents-memory-why-he-d-ban-the-chinese-models-he-uses/) (MEDIUM)

### Inferences
- MEDIUM: Coding vendors increasingly treat the repo (docs, tests, plans) as the agent's long-term memory, because it is versioned, reviewable and portable across harnesses. That matches Factory's harness-agnostic turn (section 5).
- LOW: Learning from trajectories (Cognition) is the only public example in this set of an agent improving its own process from past runs. The others improve through human-curated files.

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched on Cursor's or GitHub's memory features (Cursor rules and memories; Copilot instructions files).
- No vendor publishes evaluation data showing memory improves outcomes over time.

## 5. What have they publicly reversed or admitted was wrong?

### Takeaway
The documented reversals are: Cognition (single-agent only → structured multi-agent, 10 months later), Amp (editor extension → killed; CLI also declared temporary), Factory (co-training model and harness → model-agnostic harness), Cursor (editor-first → agent-first Cursor 3), Linear (agents as assignees → agents as delegates under a human assignee), Amp again (no model selector → a Model Selector page), Devin (Knowledge deprecated in favour of Skills), Artisan (retired "stop hiring humans", per its own blog), and 11x (customer and revenue claims, founder exit).

### Cited Findings
- **Cognition**: the 2026-04-22 post opens by acknowledging the author argued against multi-agent systems about ten months earlier: "A lot has changed since then." It keeps the warning against parallel writers. — [Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working) (HIGH)
- **Amp**: VS Code and Cursor extensions "self-destruct" on 2026-03-05; "We think the sidebar is dead" (Quinn Slack, podcast). "Amp, Rebuilt" (CLI rewrite) followed on 2026-05-04. — [Amp news](https://ampcode.com/news/the-coding-agent-is-dead) (HIGH); [Amp Chronicle](https://ampcode.com/chronicle) (MEDIUM, dates approximate)
- **Amp**: opened Claude-plan use in Amp ("By very very very very popular request"), a shift from its earlier stance of one Amp-chosen model and no bring-your-own. — [Quinn Slack on X](https://x.com/sqs/status/2108783608832122910) (MEDIUM; date not confirmed)
- **Factory**: Grinberg says he previously believed "if you train the model and you build the harness, you're going to make them better together", and now: "If you build a harness that supports different models, that harness will be better." — [Sequoia 2026-11-11](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/factorys-matan-grinberg-the-coming-dark-factory-where-software-builds-itself) (HIGH)
- **Cursor**: Cursor 3 (2026-04-02) moves from editor-first to an agent-first interface. — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/04/cursor-3-agent-first-interface/) (MEDIUM)
- **Linear**: changed assignment so an issue delegated to an agent keeps a human as primary assignee ("Assignment now reflects accountability"), around July 2025. — [Linear on X](https://x.com/linear/status/1950923480045674849) (MEDIUM); [Linear SDK post](https://linear.app/now/our-approach-to-building-the-agent-interaction-sdk) (MEDIUM)
- **11x**: TechCrunch (2025-03-24) reported claimed customers who were not customers, and employees saying "We were losing 70-80% of customers that came through the door". The company said retention was "currently 79%". Founder Hasan Sukkar stepped down about May 2025. "Artisan outgrew its own slogan … We told the world to stop hiring humans. Now we're hiring our first human BDR. - **Amp (models)**: from no model selector (2025) to a manual with a "Model Selector" page and the line "Amp uses them all". — [Amp manual](https://ampcode.com/manual) (HIGH): the selector picks the main agent's model and effort, with "Staff Pick" as the default — [Amp docs](https://ampcode.com/docs/models-and-subagents)
